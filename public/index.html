About

Software Engineering

I was a maths guy in high school. Physics too. When I got to college I did not know exactly what I wanted, but I knew it had to involve building something. Software pulled me in the moment I realised you cannot touch it but you can feel the effect of it. Code is how I turn what is in my head into something that works in the real world.

I am curious by nature. Before I write a line of code I want to understand the actual problem, which usually means talking to people about it. The RAG system I built for document search came out of sitting with medical students at Stellenbosch University's Tygerberg campus and learning that they were spending 45 minutes searching for a single clinical criterion inside a 200-page PDF. That conversation shaped every decision in the build. The best engineering comes from asking the right questions early, not from assuming you already know what the solution should look like.

I work mostly in Python across backend APIs, data pipelines, NLP systems, and ML training. I have shipped serverless infrastructure on AWS, built RAG systems grounded in real documents, taken a real client from a WhatsApp-based operation to a live production platform, and fine-tuned a language model on free-tier compute. But as much as I know how to take something from zero to deployed, I am equally comfortable joining something that already exists. I pick up patterns in a codebase quickly, learn from how the team has structured things, and contribute without breaking what is already working. I want to be in a room with engineers who are further along than me. That is where I grow fastest.

AI & Machine Learning

The area I'm deepening into is language models and applied AI: how to build real systems on top of them, how retrieval augmented generation makes models useful on actual documents, and what it takes to fine-tune a model when the data is messy and the compute budget is zero. Before writing a line of code, I interviewed medical students at Stellenbosch University's Tygerberg campus. They were spending 45 minutes searching for a single clinical criterion inside a 200-page PDF. That interview became ChunkDoc: a production RAG system with semantic retrieval, pgvector cosine similarity search, and page-level citations, grounded entirely in the uploaded document. I also built an AI Intelligence Layer that routes plain English questions across my own services, deciding which system to call and returning the answer. More recently I fine-tuned BLOOMZ-3B for Shona, one of Zimbabwe's main languages and severely underrepresented in mainstream AI, using QLoRA with 4-bit quantization on free tier compute only: Google Colab, Kaggle notebooks, and Hugging Face's free ZeroGPU tier for the live demo. I discovered and fixed an 84% data contamination issue in the training set, built a 15-prompt evaluation scorecard across 7 categories since no standard Shona benchmark exists, and shipped a full chat frontend. The project is called Muzezuru, named after the Zezuru dialect, and it is something I am continuing to develop. I use AI tools as part of my daily workflow to learn faster, move with more precision, and ship better work. The point is always to understand what I'm building, not to outsource the thinking.
